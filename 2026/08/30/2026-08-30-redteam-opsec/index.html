general opsec for RedTeams

Getting through recon clean is only step one. Most engagements don't get burned during scanning: they get burned later, during infra, C2, DNS, or just sloppy operator habits. In this post i try to cover some important steps to cover the next step of the previous started redteam opsec 'series'.

Infrastructure Segmentation

  • Tier infra by burn tolerance.
    • Long-haul C2 (redirectors, domains) → protect heavily, isolate fully
    • Short-haul (scanning boxes, phishing landers) → expect to burn, plan for it
    • Throwaway (single-use VPS, one action) → disposable by design
    • Rule: short-haul infra never shares a hosting account, SSH key, or billing method with long-haul infra. Ever.
  • Age your domains. Register months ahead of use, let them sit with a benign parked page or fake blog. A freshly registered domain gets flagged by reputation engines almost immediately: this isn’t optional anymore.
  • Pre-categorize domains with reputation services (Bluecoat, Cisco Talos, Palo Alto URL filtering) before the engagement starts, so they resolve as “business”/“technology” instead of “uncategorized”: which is itself a detection signal on a lot of proxy/SWG rulesets.
  • TLS hygiene. Let’s Encrypt or a real CA for anything client-facing. Self-signed certs on a random VPS will get you caught.

C2 Traffic Shaping

  • Domain/CDN fronting: mostly dead now. Cloudflare, AWS etc. actively detect and block it. Worth knowing how it works, not worth building a plan around.
  • Malleable C2 profiles: shape beacon traffic to look like Slack, Teams, OneDrive API calls instead of raw HTTP POSTs.
  • Jitter + sleep timers: same logic as scan-delay jitter. A static beacon interval gets caught by traffic analysis in minutes. Randomize it.
  • Match traffic volume to the mimicked protocol. Payload sizes that are too big or too small for what you’re impersonating stand out immediately.

DNS-Layer OPSEC

  • DoH for C2/exfil blends into normal DoH traffic to Cloudflare/Google resolvers: much harder for network-layer DNS monitoring to catch.
  • Low-and-slow DNS tunneling. Throttle the query rate hard. Detection keys almost entirely on volume/entropy per domain over time.
  • Your own DNS logs are a leak vector too. If you control the domain, remember the DNS provider is logging every lookup: including from defenders investigating you mid-engagement.

Endpoint & Payload OPSEC

  • LOLBins over custom binaries. PowerShell, WMI, certutil, bitsadmin: AV/EDR baseline-trusts these far more than an unknown exe.
  • In-memory execution / reflective loading. Stay off disk. Disk-scan and signature matching target file-based artifacts, not memory.
  • Know your AMSI/ETW footprint even when nothing fires live. EDR logs plenty it doesn’t alert on: plan for what ends up in a SOC’s retro-hunt data later, not just what gets actioned in real time.
  • Sleep before execution to dodge short sandbox observation windows. Trade-off: delayed execution can itself look suspicious to a human analyst reviewing the timeline after the fact.

Operator Behavioral OPSEC

  • Match time zone and working hours to whatever you’re impersonating or blending into. Don’t run ops on your own schedule if you’re posing as internal.
  • Never reuse infra across engagements. Same redirector, same JA3 fingerprint, same C2 profile across clients: that’s exactly how red team infra ends up in threat intel feeds.
  • Strip build/commit metadata. Compiler paths, usernames, internal project refs: all of it comes out before delivery. PDB paths are a classic, dumb, accidental deanonymization vector.
  • Keep attacker identity fully separate from personal identity. Dedicated VMs and personas for engagement work. No crossover in browser profiles, SSH keys, or cloud billing with anything tied to you personally.

Detection-Awareness Loop

  • Assume purple team visibility. Where the scope allows it, find out (or infer) what EDR/SIEM the target runs and calibrate technique choice to known gaps for that specific product. Stealthy against Defender is not the same as stealthy against CrowdStrike.
  • Watch for canaries/honeypots. An oddly clean, low-noise, weirdly-named host in a target range is sometimes a deliberate tripwire. Treat it with more suspicion than a normal-looking production box.
