Checking Your iPhone for Spyware with MVT

Step-by-step guide to back up an iPhone from Linux and scan the backup for spyware such as Candiru using the Mobile Verification Toolkit (MVT).

Intro

Following the recent incident in which browser ads from AdFection delivered Candiru spyware, I’m sharing a short guide to checking whether your iPhone has been infected. The process has three steps:

  1. Install the tools
  2. Back up your iPhone
  3. Scan the backup with MVT

Reading the results

MVT writes its findings to ~/mvt-results. Files ending in _detected.json contain matches against known indicators, and command.log lists any warnings. If nothing was flagged, those files will be absent or empty.

If MVT flags something, do not assume infection straight away, because false positives happen. Keep the results folder, avoid wiping the phone, and ask a digital security organization such as Amnesty’s Security Lab or Access Now’s Digital Security Helpline to review it.

Limitations

A clean result means that nothing known was found, not that your phone is definitely clean. A backup does not capture everything on the device, and a lot of iOS spyware leaves few traces. New campaigns may also not have public IOCs yet. Keep iOS up to date, and consider Lockdown Mode if you are at elevated risk.
